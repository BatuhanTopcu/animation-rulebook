# 2D craft

Applies to drawn, vector, cutout, and layered animation. A 2D delivery may depict
solid forms or intentionally flat graphics; do not impose dimensional realism
on a graphic brief. Use [styles](styles.md) to establish the visual language.

## Design poses before filling gaps

- Make the main action readable through line of action, direction, and negative
  space. Check whether a hand, face, or prop merges with the torso or background.
- Use thumbnails for the important story poses and action extremes. A breakdown
  decides the route between them: which part leads, what arcs, and what changes
  depth. It is not necessarily a geometric or temporal midpoint.
- Keep reference landmarks such as head mass, shoulder width, limb length, and
  feature placement. Check successive drawings against the chosen construction;
  distinguish intended deformation from unexplained size drift.
- Test the poses with their exposures before spending effort on clean lines.
  A polished drawing cannot rescue unclear timing.

## Spacing and exposure

Mark a landmark's intended positions along the path at the actual exposure
intervals. The spacing chart should describe acceleration, a hold, a snap, or a
change of direction. Draw toward that design rather than inserting evenly spaced
midpoints everywhere.

Keep output frame rate separate from drawing changes. Twos, threes, selective
ones, and held poses are expressive options. Use a faster pose cadence locally
when the path is otherwise unreadable; do not convert the whole shot to ones
as a generic improvement. A long exposure during a large screen displacement
can strobe. Adjust the path, spacing, exposure, smear, framing, or speed according
to the style rather than assuming a single universal fix.

A moving camera and held character drawings can produce unintended skating or
misregistration. Check their relative screen movement and layer alignment at
the composite output cadence. Matching exposures is one possible fix, not a
blanket requirement for all layers.

## Construction and deformation

Use form landmarks and perspective when the design implies volume. Maintain
overlap order as a limb moves around the body. For flat graphic shapes, preserve
the design's own shape relationships rather than inventing shading or perspective.

Compress and extend around the force or gesture. Keep an anchored contact where
needed and restore the designed form after the accent. Area preservation can
guide a flat elastic shape; an apparent-volume judgment is more appropriate for
a character turning or moving in depth. See [principles](principles.md).

In cutout animation, place pivots where the action bends and check seams at the
extremes. A rotating upper arm and a translating hand need a coherent connection.
If the puppet cannot achieve the pose, change the pose or use a supported
replacement drawing rather than stretching joints unintentionally.

## Smears, multiples, and holds

Design a smear along the action path with readable endpoints. It can connect a
fast change that discrete drawings cannot describe clearly. Keep it brief enough
for the intended cadence and judge it in playback. Avoid leaving a smear visible
as an ordinary held pose. Use a deliberate graphic smear or multiple image when
appropriate; do not assume blur is stylistically interchangeable.

A hold should retain the intention. It may be completely still, have a carefully
chosen moving hold, or sustain only an eye or breathing beat. Do not add line
boil, constant drifting, or random movement to hide a weak pose. If line boil is
part of the style, keep its cadence and amplitude consistent.

## Acting and effects

Time gaze, head, body, and gesture according to the thought. Avoid applying an
identical offset pattern to every emotion. For dialogue, preserve the intended
performance and prioritize readable phrase-level expression; mouth changes
alone do not establish acting.

For fire, smoke, water, and dust, design a force/source direction, evolving shape,
and life cycle. Successive shapes should relate to the previous state, while
overall beats can remain planned. Vary silhouettes and avoid identical pulses
unless the effect is intentionally rhythmic. Keep effect contrast below the
acting beat when it is supporting atmosphere.

## Review this medium

At playback speed, check pose readability, intended cadence, and fast accents.
Then inspect construction drift, layer seams, arcs, contact alignment, and
unintended tangencies between forms. Review the composite, not just individual
drawings. Use [review](review.md) to prioritize fixes.

For a correct drawing sequence that still strobes or loses an accent in playback,
use [perception and sampling](perception-and-sampling.md). For a technically
correct character that feels unmotivated, use [acting and dialogue](acting-and-dialogue.md).
